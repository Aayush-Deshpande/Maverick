ANUMAAN: AI-Enabled Real-Time Digital Twin for MALE UAV Aero-Piston Engines
DRDO Smart India Hackathon Problem Statement 26054
Team Midnight Ciphers

Most engine health monitoring on MALE UAVs today is just a threshold. A value crosses a line, a warning fires, and by then the fault has already happened. That is reactive, not predictive, and it is the exact gap DRDO's problem statement is pointing at. ANUMAAN is our answer: a scalable, modular, physics-first digital twin doing real-time state estimation, anomaly detection, fault diagnosis, remaining useful life estimation and mission simulation together, not five separate tools bolted side by side. We built it across five engine platforms, not one convenient demo unit, because a solution that only works on one engine is not really a solution. That list is the Rotax 912 iS, the 914 F Turbo, the 915 iS Turbo, the Austro AE300 diesel, and DRDO's own indigenous VRDE Jayem 2.2L built for Tapas-BH-201.

At the center of it sits a continuous-discrete Extended Kalman Filter running a zero and one-dimensional thermodynamic model of each engine, right alongside its live sensor data. This is what lets the twin surface things no physical sensor ever measures: in-cylinder peak pressure, turbine inlet temperature, the thickness of the oil film inside a bearing measured in microns, which is honestly the number that tells you a bearing is about to seize before anything else would. Every fault we simulate, a misfire, an overheating trend, a failing injector, sensor drift, is a genuine physical event happening inside that model, not a switch someone flipped in a script. That is the hybrid thermodynamic and data-driven approach the problem statement specifically asks for, and we think it is the only version of this that actually holds up.

The AI side never touches a raw sensor value. It only ever looks at the residual, physics-expected versus what the sensor actually reported, and only after that reading survives a sanity and cross-sensor check, essentially sensor fusion, that clears in under 40 milliseconds. Sitting on top of that residual is a bio-inspired sparse coding layer modeled on the fruit fly's sense of smell, an expand-and-sparsify trick that needs no training at all. It watches the residual every tick on modest edge hardware and catches slow joint drift across channels that no single threshold would ever flag on its own. Once something genuinely looks off, a Bayesian network takes over and isolates the responsible component against a ten-mode FMECA taxonomy built to MIL-STD-1629A, attaches a SHAP breakdown so the call comes with an actual reason attached, which is our real answer to explainable AI rather than a claim on a slide, and resolves into a fixed maintenance directive: root cause, action, emergency checklist. Same answer every time. Nothing generative in that step, on purpose.

Remaining useful life is never handed over as one number, because a single confident number in this domain is dangerous, not impressive. Two independent estimates run side by side, one from physical damage accumulation and one from residual drift trending, and the combined result sits inside a split-conformal-calibrated range with a real statistical guarantee behind it. Mission planning only ever trusts the conservative low end of that range. ANUMAAN also works out mission reliability, the actual odds a sortie finishes without a propulsion abort, by running the mission forward many times with randomized failure draws phase by phase, and it keeps a live glide-reachability footprint running for divert decisions, plus mission replay of past flights for anyone reviewing afterward.

All of this reaches the operator through the ground control station. A browser-based 3D twin for each engine with fault-synced part highlighting, and separate pilot and propulsion-engineer views, because those two people need the same truth shown very differently. Telemetry gets there over a CAN bus edge link, the kind of interface that would sit right behind a real ECU or FADEC. Fleet learning runs through federated updates, one of the innovation areas the problem statement names directly, and it never centralizes raw telemetry and is differentially private by design, so one base's conditions can shape the shared model without ever leaking patrol patterns or mission tempo.

What we are actually delivering is a working prototype, end to end, not something stitched together for a demo video. Physics-based state estimation, AI fault detection, calibrated prognostics and mission reliability, all running live across all five engines, with full documentation and an honest roadmap for the certification work that is still left to do.
