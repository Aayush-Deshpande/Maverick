## INTERROGATION METHODOLOGY

This document simulates a high-intensity, adversarial oral defense before a panel of senior DRDO propulsion scientists, military avionics certification directors (CEMILAC), and aerospace systems specialists. 

The cross-examination follows a **progressive escalation model**: the evaluator begins with fundamental terminology, probes the physical data connections, challenges scientific assumptions, attacks mathematical circularities, and culminates in deep airworthiness and systems failure modes.

For every interrogation round, the analysis records:
1. **The Question Asked by the Judge**
2. **What the Judge Is Actually Testing (Underlying Trap)**
3. **Our Current Defensible Answer**
4. **Evidence on Disk (File Paths & Line Numbers)**
5. **Our Technical Weakness / Vulnerability**
6. **The Inevitable Follow-Up Question**
7. **How We Should Improve Our Implementation & Answer**

### ROUND 1: THE DIGITAL TWIN DEFINITION TRAP

#### 1. Question
> *"You call this a Digital Twin. What is your definition of a Digital Twin, and how is your system fundamentally different from a standard simulation running in a loop?"*

#### 2. What the Judge Is Actually Testing
The judge is testing whether the team understands the formal aerospace definitions (**AIAA S-119-2011**, **ISO 23247**, **NASA-STD-7009A**) or whether they are simply using "Digital Twin" as a marketing buzzword for a 3D graphic.

#### 3. Our Current Defensible Answer `[PARTIALLY IMPLEMENTED]`
*"A simulation runs forward in isolation based on initial conditions. Under ISO 23247, our implementation is a **Digital Shadow operating at STANAG 4586 Level of Interoperability (LOI) 2**. It maintains an active, time-synchronized 7D physical state vector ($\hat{\mathbf{x}}_k$) updated at 20 Hz from telemetry. It runs a thermodynamic observer model in parallel with the aircraft, continuously computing expected physical values. By calculating the difference between measured and expected states, it generates **thermodynamic residuals** that dynamically update material shaders on a 109-part CAD mesh and feed an anomaly detection autoencoder. It is an observer-twin, not an isolated simulation."*

#### 4. Evidence on Disk
* `backend/services/state.py` (20 Hz tick loop updating `EngineStateService`);
* `backend/physics/thermo_model.py` (`step_physics()` observer);
* `Report/06_digital_twin_audit.md`.

#### 5. Weakness / Vulnerability
We do not have bi-directional closed-loop control back to the engine ECU actuators.

#### 6. The Inevitable Follow-Up Question
> *"If it's an observer, show me exactly where the physical system state enters your Digital Twin code."*

#### 7. How to Improve
Acknowledge the LOI 2 read-only boundary proactively and justify it using **DO-178C flight safety**: closing the loop to allow automated AI control over throttle in flight violates civil and military airworthiness certification.

### ROUND 3: RESIDUALS VS. SENSOR DRIFT VS. ENGINE DAMAGE

#### 1. Question
> *"When your measured state disagrees with your physics model, how do you determine whether the discrepancy is caused by sensor drift, model inaccuracy, or actual mechanical engine degradation?"*

#### 2. What the Judge Is Actually Testing
This is a master-level systems engineering question. The judge is testing for **sensor validation vs. plant degradation observability** and whether the system suffers from false alarms due to sensor bias.

#### 3. Our Current Defensible Answer `[IMPLEMENTED]`
*"We use a **Multi-Channel Thermodynamic Correlation Matrix and Rate-of-Change Gating**:
1. **Sensor Failure (Electrical Open Circuit):** A broken thermocouple causes an instantaneous step change ($dT/dt > 500^\circ\text{C/s}$) dropping to 0 or spiking to maximum ADC rail. Cylinder heads have high thermal mass ($C_p$) and cannot physically change temperature faster than $5^\circ\text{C/s}$. The plausibility filter rejects this immediately as `SENSOR_FAULT`.
2. **Sensor Drift:** If a single thermocouple drifts upward slowly, only its single-channel residual spikes. Adjacent cylinder heads, exhaust gas temperatures (EGT), and oil temperatures remain completely nominal. The Random Forest classifier explicitly isolates single-channel divergence as **Fault F05 (Sensor Drift)**.
3. **Genuine Mechanical Engine Degradation:** A physical fault exhibits multi-sensor thermodynamic coupling. For example, an exhaust valve blowout (Fault F02) causes CHT to climb, EGT to drop, oil temperature to rise slightly, and 0.5X crank vibration to spike. The multi-variable correlation proves physical degradation rather than sensor drift."*

#### 4. Evidence on Disk
* `backend/services/detection_pipeline.py` (Plausibility & rate-of-change checks);
* `backend/ml/classifier.py` (Random Forest multi-channel split logic);
* `Report/08_fault_detection_prediction_rul.md`.

#### 5. Weakness / Vulnerability
If two redundant thermocouples drift simultaneously in the same direction, the system could misclassify the drift as thermal degradation.

#### 6. The Inevitable Follow-Up Question
> *"What training data taught your Random Forest to distinguish between sensor drift and valve failure? Where did those labels come from?"*

#### 7. How to Improve
Incorporate an analytical redundancy voting block in the telemetry preprocessor that compares dual-lane ECU A vs ECU B sensor values before feature extraction.

### ROUND 4: THE TRAINING CIRCULARITY ATTACK

#### 1. Question
> *"You claim 97.5% classification accuracy. But your training data was generated by `RotaxTimeSeriesGenerator`, which imports your own `RotaxThermoModel`. Isn't your AI model completely circular—just memorizing your own equations?"*

#### 2. What the Judge Is Actually Testing
The evaluator has inspected the imports in `backend/ml/generator.py` and caught the ultimate scientific trap: the model was evaluated on data produced by the exact same simulator it is supposed to monitor.

#### 3. Our Current Defensible Answer `[EXPERIMENTAL / AUDITED]`
*"Yes, that is an accurate forensic observation, and we acknowledge it with total scientific honesty. In `backend/ml/generator.py`, synthetic telemetry is generated using `RotaxThermoModel` with injected fault offsets. This is a **simulation-in-the-loop cold-start methodology**. 
In military aerospace programs where destructive in-flight failure data (such as deliberately seizing a piston or cutting oil lines during flight) does not exist in sufficient quantities, physics-based fault injection is standard industry practice (identical to NASA C-MAPSS and DARPA Digital Twin frameworks).
However, we do not feed raw values to the classifier; we feed **residuals**. To prevent simple memorization, the generator injects non-stationary Gaussian noise, random atmospheric lapse rates, and varying pilot throttle profiles. We present the 97.5% score as proof that the residual feature space cleanly separates the 8 fault topologies under synthetic conditions."*

#### 4. Evidence on Disk
* `backend/ml/generator.py` (`NUM_MISSIONS = 30`, `MISSION_DURATION = 45`);
* `Report/02_complete_code_audit.md` (Section 4.1);
* `Report/07_ai_ml_audit.md`.

#### 5. Weakness / Vulnerability
The entire training dataset is only 22.5 minutes long ($30 \times 45\text{ s}$).

#### 6. The Inevitable Follow-Up Question
> *"Twenty-two minutes of data? An endurance MALE UAV flies for 24 hours in a single sortie. How can you claim airworthiness on twenty-two minutes of synthetic data?"*

#### 7. How to Improve
Run `ACT-04` immediately to generate a 500-hour multi-mission stochastic dataset spanning extreme temperature envelopes ($-40^\circ\text{C}$ to $+55^\circ\text{C}$, 0 to 30,000 ft) and retrain the classifier before the final presentation.

#### 2. What the Judge Is Actually Testing

*"You are entirely correct about `backend/ml/rul_estimator.py`, and we are glad you caught it. That was an initial placeholder script created during rapid prototyping, and it represents a technical debt item that is being deleted.
Our **real, statistically grounded prognostics engine is implemented in `backend/ml/trend_analyser.py`**. 
In `trend_analyser.py`, we do not use static countdowns:
1. We fit linear and quadratic degradation polynomials to historical thermal residuals.
2. We evaluate goodness-of-fit using the **Akaike Information Criterion (AIC)**.
3. We execute a **500-sample Monte Carlo perturbation** across parameter covariance matrices.
4. We project when the residual will breach the critical metallurgical safety threshold ($150^\circ\text{C}$), reporting median RUL with **90% confidence intervals**.
5. Concurrently, a **Rainflow cycle counting** algorithm tracks low-cycle thermal fatigue damage fraction ($D$).
`rul_estimator.py` is obsolete and scheduled for immediate deletion."*

### ROUND 7: THE 10 kHz VIBRATION DOWNLINK PARADOX

#### 1. Question
> *"You claim to process 10 kHz vibration data for gearbox and bearing health. A 10 kHz 16-bit 3-axis accelerometer produces 480 kbit/s. A military UAV Ku-band satellite downlink allocates only 25 kbit/s for engine engineering data. How do you get that data to the ground?"*

#### 2. What the Judge Is Actually Testing
The judge is testing whether the team understands avionics link budgets and edge-versus-ground processing boundaries, or if they naively assumed infinite internet bandwidth.

#### 3. Our Current Defensible Answer `[IMPLEMENTED / ARCHITECTURAL]`
*"We do not stream raw vibration data over the air link. That would be an engineering impossibility that would saturate the C2 link.
Our architecture enforces a strict **Edge-to-Ground Functional Partition**:
1. **Onboard Edge Processor:** The 10 kHz accelerometer is sampled locally by an embedded DSP chip. It performs tach-synchronized order tracking, windowed FFT, and envelope demodulation directly on the aircraft.
2. **Telemetry Downlink:** The edge computer compresses the high-frequency vibration spectrum into **four scalar feature metrics**:
   * Overall Vibration RMS ($g$)
   * Crest Factor
   * 1X Crankshaft Order Amplitude ($g$)
   * Gearbox Tooth Mesh Order Amplitude ($g$)
   These four float16 values consume only **64 bits per frame**. At 10 Hz reporting, that is **640 bits per second**—well within our 25 kbit/s Ku-band budget."*

#### 4. Evidence on Disk
* `docs/study/04_telemetry_and_comms.md`;
* `docs/study/06_vibration_analysis.md`;
* `Report/05_target_architecture.md`.

#### 5. Weakness / Vulnerability
In the current code, the 10 kHz vibration DSP runs in Python simulation rather than compiled C99 on an onboard micro-controller.

#### 6. The Inevitable Follow-Up Question
> *"Have you compiled and benchmarked that FFT order-tracking routine on an actual embedded ARM Cortex or DSP chip?"*

#### 7. How to Improve
Cite the ARM CMSIS-DSP library benchmark: a 1024-point real FFT on an ARM Cortex-M7 running at 480 MHz executes in **42 microseconds**, consuming less than 0.5% of the microcontroller's CPU budget.

#### 1. Question

#### 2. What the Judge Is Actually Testing
The evaluator is testing software airworthiness compliance (CEMILAC DDPMAS / RTCA DO-178C) and whether the team understands the difference between flight-critical airborne avionics and ground support tools.

#### 3. Our Current Defensible Answer `[AUDITED]`
*"We make a strict architectural distinction between airborne flight control and ground decision support:
1. **The Ground Station is DO-178C DAL-E:** Our FastAPI backend and React frontend run entirely on the Ground Control Station (GCS). Under **STANAG 4586 Level of Interoperability (LOI) 2**, our software is an advisory telemetry observer with zero control authority. As a ground-based diagnostic and advisory tool, it falls under DAL-E, where Python's high productivity and async ecosystem are fully permitted.
2. **The Airborne Edge Daemon Roadmap:** For the onboard data collector that interfaces directly with the aircraft CAN bus, we have architected a lightweight C99 daemon running on a certified RTOS (such as PikeOS or FreeRTOS) that isolates the telemetry transmitter via a hardware data diode, preventing any software fault on our system from affecting the flight-critical Full Authority Digital Engine Control (FADEC)."*

#### 4. Evidence on Disk
* `Report/05_target_architecture.md`;
* `Report/12_drdo_defence_aerospace_gap_analysis.md` (Section 3.1).

#### 5. Weakness / Vulnerability

#### 6. The Inevitable Follow-Up Question
